# Departed users strand sheets, not reports

When a Qlik Sense user leaves, **their community sheets are not deleted on either platform, and consumers should keep seeing them. Reports that depend on the departed user's ownership are what break.** In Qlik Cloud, deleting a user leaves their apps, spaces, scripts and data connections "in the tenant without an owner" ([Qlik Help](https://help.qlik.com/en-US/cloud-services/Subsystems/Hub/Content/Sense_Hub/Admin/SaaS-delete-users.htm)), and their private and community app objects stay inside the app as orphaned objects. In client-managed Qlik Sense Enterprise on Windows (QSEoW), deleting the user in the QMC moves ownership of everything they owned to the internal `sa_repository` account ([Qlik Help](https://help.qlik.com/en-US/sense-admin/May2025/Subsystems/DeployAdministerQSE/Content/Sense_DeployAdminister/QSEoW/Administer_QSEoW/Managing_QSEoW/delete-users.htm)). Approved (base) sheets belong to the app and are unaffected on both platforms. Qlik does not document anywhere that consumers can still view a departed user's community sheets. That conclusion comes from how access rules work and from customer reports, so it needs a lab test before anyone promises it to a customer. Reporting is a different story. In Qlik Cloud, the user's **alerts and automations are deleted** with the user, **report tasks stop when the owner is disabled**, and subscriptions and report tasks have to be re-owned by an admin before offboarding. Reports owned by other people that point at the departed user's community sheet should keep working as long as the sheet stays public with the same object ID. They fail if cleanup deletes the sheet, makes it private or re-creates it. The safest pattern is to **approve the sheets people rely on, re-own reports and alerts, and only then disable, delete and remove the user from the IdP**.

## The short answer Anand can paste to Sruthi

> Short version: the sheets don't disappear, but plan the offboarding so reporting doesn't break.
>
> - **Community sheets are not deleted when a user leaves.** In Qlik Cloud, deleting the user leaves their community and private sheets in the app with no owner. On client-managed (QSEoW), deleting the user in the QMC moves ownership to the internal sa_repository account. Approved/base sheets belong to the app and aren't affected. Just disabling the user, or removing them from AD/the IdP, doesn't change sheet ownership at all.
> - **Consumers should still be able to see the community sheets**, because access follows the space/stream rather than the sheet owner. Qlik doesn't state this explicitly, so I'd test it once. What does break is maintenance: nobody can edit, unpublish or delete those sheets from the UI any more, and private sheets are invisible to everyone. Admins can recover private sheets with the QMC owner edit (client-managed) or an OAuth bot with the admin.apps scope (Cloud).
> - **Reporting is the real risk.** In Qlik Cloud, alerts and automations the user owned are deleted when the user is deleted, and report tasks they owned stop running once they're disabled. Have an admin change the owner of their subscriptions, report tasks and alerts under Administration first. Reports owned by other people that use the sheet keep working as long as the sheet stays public and isn't deleted or recreated. NPrinting runs under its connection identity, so it's fine unless the departed user *was* that identity.
> - **Recommendation:** before offboarding, have the app owner approve any community sheets people rely on (this makes them base sheets), re-own reports, alerts, automations and apps, then disable or deallocate the license, delete the user, and remove them from the IdP.

How to read the rest of this report: statements followed by a citation are documented by Qlik (help, qlik.dev or official support articles) unless they are labelled *community report* or *anecdotal*. Statements labelled **Inference** are reasoning from those sources and are untested. The lab-test table near the end lists every inference that someone should verify before relying on it.

## Deletion orphans sheets in Cloud but hands them to sa_repository on Windows

The two platforms handle a departing owner in opposite ways, and on both the word "orphaned" means something narrower than it sounds. In **Qlik Cloud**, deletion is the step that orphans content. The official deletion page says owned spaces, apps, scripts and data connections remain without an owner and "must be reassigned," and that "Notifications and alerts owned by the user are deleted from the system" and "Automations and automation connections owned by the user are deleted from the system" ([Qlik Help](https://help.qlik.com/en-US/cloud-services/Subsystems/Hub/Content/Sense_Hub/Admin/SaaS-delete-users.htm)). The same page says nothing about sheets, bookmarks or stories. Qlik's own operational guidance fills the gap: "Inactive user's sheets are often orphaned, unless they have a published status of `true`," and sheets with approved status "cannot be orphaned" ([qlik.dev](https://www.qlik.dev/manage/oem/operate/maintenance/)). Qlik Support lists "orphaned personal objects (owner deleted)" as reassignable, which means they still exist in the app ([Qlik Support](https://community.qlik.com/t5/Official-Support-Articles/Qlik-Cloud-Analytics-Is-it-Possible-to-Recover-a-Deleted-App-or/ta-p/2428385)). A customer comment on that article (anecdotal, February 2025) reports that after IdP-driven deletions, "private and community content (sheets, bookmarks, stories) were NOT DELETED" ([Qlik Support comments](https://community.qlik.com/t5/Official-Support-Articles/Qlik-Cloud-Analytics-Is-it-Possible-to-Recover-a-Deleted-App-or/ta-p/2428385)). Disabling a user or removing their entitlement orphans nothing: "Removing a user's entitlement does not affect any resources owned by the user" ([Qlik Help](https://help.qlik.com/en-US/cloud-services/Subsystems/Hub/Content/Sense_Hub/Admin/SaaS-user-allocations.htm)). Removing a user from the IdP alone also changes nothing in Qlik. Worse, if a user is deleted in Qlik but not in the IdP, their next login creates **a new tenant user record with a new ID**, possibly with an automatic license assignment ([Qlik Help](https://help.qlik.com/en-US/cloud-services/Subsystems/Hub/Content/Sense_Hub/Admin/SaaS-delete-users.htm)). **Inference:** that returning user does not get their old sheets back, because objects stay tied to the old user ID.

In **QSEoW**, nothing becomes ownerless. When a user disappears from AD/LDAP, the next User Directory Connector sync flags them "Removed externally" and inactivates them. An admin can also set an inactive user to "Blocked". Either kind of inactive user **keeps ownership of all their objects** and their custom properties ([Qlik Help](https://help.qlik.com/en-US/sense-admin/May2025/Subsystems/DeployAdministerQSE/Content/Sense_DeployAdminister/QSEoW/Administer_QSEoW/Managing_QSEoW/inactivate-users.htm)). Only when an admin deletes the user in the QMC does "the ownership of the user's objects [move] to the sa_repository user," and that behaviour is unchanged since Qlik Sense 1.1 ([Qlik Help](https://help.qlik.com/en-US/sense-admin/May2025/Subsystems/DeployAdministerQSE/Content/Sense_DeployAdminister/QSEoW/Administer_QSEoW/Managing_QSEoW/delete-users.htm); [Qlik Sense 1.1 help](https://help.qlik.com/sense/1.1/en-US/online/Subsystems/ManagementConsole/Content/QMC_Resources_Users_DeletingUsers.htm)). Community and personal sheets live physically inside the app's .qvf in the share storage ([Qlik Support](https://community.qlik.com/t5/Official-Support-Articles/How-to-backup-migrate-or-import-community-and-personal-sheets-to/tac-p/2419491)). **Inference:** the real threat to them is an operation on the app, such as deleting it or republishing under a new app ID, rather than anything done to the user. Treat any claim that QSEoW automatically purges "Removed externally" users as unverified. The research found deletion documented only as a manual QMC or QRS action, or as an option when deleting the UDC itself.

| Event | Qlik Cloud | QSEoW (client-managed) |
|---|---|---|
| Removed from IdP / AD only | No change in Qlik; the user record persists. If the user was deleted in Qlik but not in the IdP, they can log in again as a new user (documented) | UDC sync flags "Removed externally" (inactive); user keeps ownership of all objects (documented) |
| Disabled / blocked / entitlement removed | User record and ownership intact; entitlement removed (documented). Alerts reportedly disabled (help snippet, not re-verified) | Blocked user keeps ownership (documented) |
| Deleted | Apps/spaces/scripts/connections ownerless; alerts and automations deleted (documented). Private/community sheets remain as orphaned objects (qlik.dev + Support; no explicit help statement) | All owned objects move to `sa_repository`; custom properties deleted (documented) |
| Approved/base sheets | "Cannot be orphaned" (qlik.dev) | Belong to the app; unapproving returns the sheet to its original owner (documented) |

## Consumers keep seeing community sheets, but nobody can maintain them

On visibility, the evidence is strong but indirect on both platforms. In QSEoW, the default Stream security rule grants read on published app objects to anyone who can read the stream. It tests that the object is published and that the user has stream rights, and **it never tests the owner** ([Qlik Support](https://support.qlik.com/articles/000046126); [Qlik Support](https://community.qlik.com/t5/Official-Support-Articles/Hierarchical-relationships-in-Security-Rules-for-Qlik-Sense/ta-p/1711781/highlight/true)). **Inference:** a published community sheet stays visible to stream consumers whether its owner is active, inactive or replaced by `sa_repository`. The exception is custom rules keyed on `resource.owner` (for example `resource.owner.@Department` or `resource.owner.userDirectory`). After deletion those rules see `sa_repository`, which has no custom properties, so they can silently grant or deny differently. Audit them before bulk-deleting users. In Qlik Cloud, Qlik's own description of these objects as "orphaned" rather than deleted, plus the customer report above, points the same way. No Qlik page confirms that consumers in a managed space can still open a deleted user's community sheet, so this is the **number-one item to lab-test**.

Maintenance is what breaks. On QSEoW, a community reply says community sheets "are deletable only by its owner" through the hub, though an admin can act on them in QMC App objects ([Qlik Community](https://community.qlik.com/t5/New-to-Qlik-Analytics/How-can-an-administrator-delete-a-community-sheet-published-by-a/m-p/1156612)). In Qlik Cloud, published sheets cannot be modified and must be unpublished before deletion, and "a simple unpublish option cannot be done with the Qlik REST API" or Qlik Automate ([Qlik Support](https://community.qlik.com/t5/Official-Support-Articles/Qlik-Cloud-Analytics-Can-you-unpublish-community-sheets-with-a/ta-p/2518170)). Consumers with sufficient permissions can still duplicate a published sheet, which creates a private copy they own ([Qlik Help](https://help.qlik.com/en-us/cloud-services/Subsystems/Hub/Content/Sense_Hub/Share/make-public-or-private.htm)). The copy has a new object ID, which matters for reports (next section).

Ownership transfer is solved on QSEoW and possible only by script on Cloud. In the **QMC**, App objects supports multi-select and an Owner field in the Identification section, so one edit can reassign every object a departed user owns ([Qlik Help](https://help.qlik.com/en-US/sense-admin/May2025/Subsystems/DeployAdministerQSE/Content/Sense_DeployAdminister/QSEoW/Administer_QSEoW/Managing_QSEoW/edit-app-objects.htm); [Qlik Community](https://community.qlik.com/t5/New-to-Qlik-Analytics/Batch-change-ownership-of-app-objects-in-Qlik-Sense/m-p/1800764)). The QRS equivalent is `GET`/`PUT /qrs/app/object/{id}` with a new `owner.id` ([Qlik Community](https://community.qlik.com/t5/Integration-Extension-APIs/Change-ownership-of-Qlik-Sense-object-using-QRS-API/m-p/1780770)). The community-maintained Qlik-Cli-Windows module wraps this call as `Update-QlikObject -owner`, and its `-approved` switch sets the approved flag the same way ([GitHub](https://github.com/ahaydon/Qlik-Cli-Windows/blob/master/resources/object.ps1)). Do this **before** deleting the user. Afterwards every departed user's objects sit under `sa_repository`, and the owner field no longer tells you whose they were. Private sheets reassigned in the QMC reportedly show up for the new owner (community reports, not official docs) ([Qlik Community](https://community.qlik.com/t5/New-to-Qlik-Analytics/How-to-access-another-user-s-duplicated-sheets-that-have-been/m-p/1808889)). One related caution: in a 2021 case, changing an *app's* owner in the QMC moved all the previous owner's private sheets with it ([Qlik Community](https://community.qlik.com/t5/Management-Governance/Losing-private-sheets/td-p/1791845)).

In **Qlik Cloud**, admins can change the owner of spaces, apps, scripts and data connections in Administration (filter Owner = "Deleted User") ([Qlik Support](https://community.qlik.com/t5/Official-Support-Articles/Qlik-Cloud-Analytics-Can-the-owner-of-orphaned-Apps-be-changed/ta-p/2484034)). The UI cannot change the owner of a sheet. Changing the app owner does not carry private sheets across; Qlik Support tested it and found "the private sheets are not saved" ([Qlik Community](https://community.qlik.com/t5/Move-to-Qlik-Cloud-Analytics/Anyone-ever-found-a-way-to-recover-private-sheets-that-belong-to/m-p/2496045)). The supported route since **March 2024** is an OAuth M2M "private content bot" with the `admin.apps` scope. It can list, publish, duplicate and reassign other users' private and orphaned objects through `POST /v1/apps/{appId}/objects/{objectId}/actions/change-owner` or `qlik app object change-owner` ([qlik.dev changelog 86](https://qlik.dev/changelog/86-m2m-access-personal-metadata/); [qlik.dev tutorial](https://www.qlik.dev/manage/platform-operations/private-content-bots.md); [Qlik Support](https://community.qlik.com/t5/Official-Support-Articles/Qlik-Cloud-Analytics-Is-it-Possible-to-Recover-a-Deleted-App-or/ta-p/2428385)). An interactive admin's own token is still limited to "You must own the object to change it's ownership" ([qlik.dev changelog 38](https://qlik.dev/changelog/38-api-updates-changeowner/)). The research notes disagree on one point. The client-managed researcher, relying on a December 2024 thread, describes Cloud private-sheet recovery as impossible. The same thread later confirms the M2M approach works, so the later evidence wins. The CLI listing has a practical catch: Support warns "We currently don't show the current owner of each object," so finding a departed user's objects means scripting Engine API `qMeta.owner` against the Users API ([Qlik Support](https://community.qlik.com/t5/Official-Support-Articles/Qlik-Cloud-Analytics-Is-it-Possible-to-Recover-a-Deleted-App-or/ta-p/2428385)). Qlik's August 2026 Q&A still describes taking over a departed colleague's sheets as "script-based," not a UI flow ([Qlik Community](https://community.qlik.com/t5/Video-Transcripts/Q-amp-A-with-Qlik-New-to-Qlik-Cloud-August-2026/ta-p/2554681)).

**Approval is the strongest mitigation on both platforms.** On QSEoW, Qlik says that when a sheet is unapproved, "If the sheet belonged to another user, they will become the owner again," which implies the app owns approved sheets ([Qlik Help May 2026](https://help.qlik.com/en-US/sense/May2026/Subsystems/Hub/Content/Sense_Hub/Publishing/unapproving-sheets.htm)). qlik.dev says only non-base objects can have an owner different from the app ([qlik.dev](https://qlik.dev/manage/migrate/qlik-cli-app-objects-migrate/)). **Inference:** approving a community sheet removes any dependence on the departed user and avoids the unpublish-reassign-republish cycle, which would briefly make the sheet private and break reports. Three things are not documented: whether approval preserves the object ID, whether approval still works on a Cloud sheet whose owner was deleted, and whether QRS honours `approved=true` on an unpublished sheet.

## Reports fail on the report owner, not on the sheet owner

The pattern across the reporting stack is that **each report runs as its owner (or as a service identity), not as the author of the sheet it renders.** That divides the risk in two. Reports *owned by* the departed user are the urgent problem. Reports owned by someone else that *point at* the departed user's community sheet keep working until somebody cleans the sheet up.

In Qlik Cloud, "When a user creates a subscription, they become the owner." Tenant and analytics admins can edit subscriptions and in-app report tasks, change their owner, and disable or delete them from the Subscriptions administration page ([Qlik Help](https://help.qlik.com/en-US/cloud-services/Subsystems/Hub/Content/Sense_Hub/Admin/mc-administer-report-subscription-items.htm)). The user-deletion page is silent on subscriptions and report tasks. A May 2025 Qlik Support exchange, however, confirms that report task ownership does **not** follow an app owner change, that "the report won't run if I disable the employee," and that the fix is to change the owner on the admin Subscriptions page ([Qlik Community](https://community.qlik.com/t5/Reporting-Service-Alerting/Change-report-ownership/td-p/2519358)). **Disabled is therefore a safe holding state for sheets but not for report tasks.** Two documented traps affect the new owner. In apps with section access, an admin owner change automatically disables the report task, and "only the new report task owner can re-enable the report task." And "External report recipients receive report output as defined by the access of the report task owner," so a new owner with different section access silently changes what external recipients get ([Qlik Help](https://help.qlik.com/en-US/cloud-services/Subsystems/Hub/Content/Sense_Hub/Reporting/tabular-reports-section-access.htm)). Alerts are deleted outright when their owner is deleted, so admins must use "Change the owner of an alert" **before** deletion. The new owner must have access to the app, and needs a Professional entitlement for multi-recipient alerts ([Qlik Help](https://help.qlik.com/en-US/cloud-services/Subsystems/Hub/Content/Sense_Hub/Admin/mc-managing-alerts.htm)). Automations are also deleted with their owner per the help page ([Qlik Help](https://help.qlik.com/en-US/cloud-services/Subsystems/Hub/Content/Sense_Hub/Admin/SaaS-delete-users.htm)). A Support KB contradicts this by still listing "orphaned automations" as recoverable ([Qlik Support](https://community.qlik.com/t5/Official-Support-Articles/Qlik-Cloud-Analytics-Is-it-Possible-to-Recover-a-Deleted-App-or/ta-p/2428385)), so plan on the help page being right. The only documented transfer, `qlik automation change-owner`, is marked deprecated. It "removes the history and change logs," and "All linked connections used in the automation are detached and not moved to the new owner" ([qlik.dev](https://qlik.dev/toolkits/qlik-cli/automation/automation-change-owner/)).

Reports owned by active users that reference the departed user's community sheet depend on the sheet staying put. "Subscriptions follow the same access rules as applications, data, and spaces" ([Qlik Help](https://help.qlik.com/en-US/cloud-services/Subsystems/Hub/Content/Sense_Hub/Reporting/Subscription-reports.htm)), so an orphaned but still-public sheet should render. **This is an inference**; no Qlik source tests it. The documented failure modes all come from cleanup. If any sheet fails to export, no report is sent, "A sheet has been deleted" is a listed cause, and "If a subscription fails to generate three times, it will be turned off" ([Qlik Help](https://help.qlik.com/en-US/cloud-services/Subsystems/Hub/Content/Sense_Hub/Reporting/Subscription-reports.htm)). For tabular reports, "Making a public sheet used in a tabular report private causes the report task to fail," and template object IDs must match the app ([Qlik Support](https://community.qlik.com/t5/Official-Support-Articles/Qlik-Tabular-Reporting-General-Troubleshooting-and-Best/ta-p/2160224)). That is why duplicating or re-creating the departed user's sheet is not a neutral fix: it produces new object IDs that break existing templates. Departed users left on recipient lists also cause failures. "Filter group has no access to app" appears when a recipient is a disabled tenant user ([Qlik Support](https://community.qlik.com/t5/Official-Support-Articles/Qlik-Tabular-Reporting-General-Troubleshooting-and-Best/ta-p/2160224)). If anyone deletes and restores the app, "Associated content, such as collections, tags, data alerts, subscriptions, and notes, is permanently lost" ([Qlik Support](https://community.qlik.com/t5/Official-Support-Articles/Qlik-Cloud-Analytics-Is-it-Possible-to-Recover-a-Deleted-App-or/ta-p/2428385)).

**NPrinting** on client-managed is the most robust of the lot because it ignores object authorship. Each connection runs as a configured Identity, "the <Windows domain name>\<user name> of the Qlik Sense user who has access to the app," over certificates ([Qlik NPrinting Help](https://help.qlik.com/en-US/nprinting/Content/NPrinting/GettingStarted/HowCreateConnections/Connect-to-QlikSense-apps.htm)). Templates reference objects by ID ([Qlik Community](https://community.qlik.com/t5/Qlik-NPrinting/Find-Sense-object-in-app-based-on-object-id/td-p/2132536)). **Inference:** a report built on objects from a departed user's community sheet keeps working while the object exists and the connection identity can see it. It breaks if the sheet is deleted, re-created, made private or hidden by a custom owner-based rule. It also breaks if the departed user *was* the connection Identity or the NPrinting service account, or appears in a section access table NPrinting relies on. With section access, "The Qlik Sense app and individual app objects must be published" ([Qlik NPrinting Help](https://help.qlik.com/en-US/nprinting/Content/NPrinting/DeployingQVNprinting/Section-access.htm)), and Qlik's object-support KB recommends removing private sheets from published apps ([Qlik Community KB](https://community.qlik.com/t5/Knowledge/Qlik-NPrinting-and-Qlik-Sense-Object-Support-and-Limitations/ta-p/1717018)). The research did not cover QSEoW-native alerting or other on-premises distribution tools, so treat those as open.

| Mechanism | If the departed user OWNS it | If someone else owns it and it uses the departed user's community sheet |
|---|---|---|
| Cloud subscription | Admin can change owner (documented); behaviour on deletion undocumented | Should deliver while the sheet stays public (inference); fails if the sheet is deleted, off after 3 failures (documented) |
| Cloud report task (tabular/in-app) | Stops once the owner is disabled (Support thread); admin re-owns; section-access tasks need re-enabling by the new owner (documented) | Fails if the sheet is made private or object IDs change (documented) |
| Cloud alert | Deleted with the owner; change owner first (documented) | Not researched |
| Cloud automation | Deleted with the owner (help); transfer CLI deprecated and lossy (documented) | Not researched |
| NPrinting (QSEoW) | Breaks if the user was the connection Identity or service account (inference from docs) | Works while the object exists and is visible to the Identity (inference) |

## Licenses free up on delete in Cloud but need manual release on Windows

In Qlik Cloud, "Deleting an active or disabled user frees up a user entitlement." Removing an entitlement leaves owned resources untouched, but dynamic assignment re-grants it at the next login, so **disable the user** to stop that ([Qlik Help](https://help.qlik.com/en-US/cloud-services/Subsystems/Hub/Content/Sense_Hub/Admin/SaaS-delete-users.htm); [Qlik Help](https://help.qlik.com/en-US/cloud-services/Subsystems/Hub/Content/Sense_Hub/Admin/SaaS-user-allocations.htm)). A help snippet, not re-verified, says the disabled status removes the entitlement ([Qlik Help](https://help.qlik.com/en-US/cloud-services/Content/Sense_Hub/Admin/SaaS-user-entitlements-capacity-model.htm)). **Inference:** if so, keeping a departed user disabled while content is triaged costs no seat. Consumers viewing the orphaned sheets need their own entitlement, not the author's. A 2023 defect left licenses assigned to deleted users; it was fixed in March 2023 and can be cleared with `qlik license assignment delete` ([Qlik Support](https://community.qlik.com/t5/Official-Support-Articles/Qlik-Cloud-license-remains-assigned-to-deleted-user/ta-p/2051581)).

On QSEoW, **an allocation is not released when a user leaves AD**. The Admin Playbook says to filter Users by Inactive = Yes, deallocate Professional and Analyzer access manually, and check auto-allocating license rules ([Qlik Admin Playbook](https://help.qlik.com/en-US/qs-admin-playbook/Content/licensing/license_maintenance.htm)). If the user logged in within the last seven days, deallocated professional access goes into a **7-day quarantine**. Otherwise it is released immediately ([Qlik Help, April 2019](https://help.qlik.com/en-US/sense/april2019/Subsystems/ManagementConsole/Content/Sense_QMC/manage-professional-access.htm)). That wording comes from 2019 help and was not re-confirmed for 2024–2026 releases. **Inference:** deallocation affects access, not content; ownership changes only on user deletion. Whether deleting a user automatically frees their allocation is undocumented.

## An offboarding checklist that re-owns content before deleting the user

Order matters more than any single step. Reassignment is easiest while the user still exists, because Cloud alerts and automations are gone once the user is deleted, and on QSEoW all ownership merges into `sa_repository`.

| # | Step | Qlik Cloud | QSEoW (client-managed) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Inventory everything the user owns | Administration: spaces, apps, scripts, data connections, subscriptions/report tasks, alerts; script Engine `qMeta.owner` per app for sheets (no UI report exists) | QMC App objects filtered by owner; apps; NPrinting connection identities and recipient lists |
| 2 | Re-own reports first | Change owner of subscriptions, report tasks and alerts; new owner re-enables section-access report tasks; check what external recipients will now receive | Repoint NPrinting connections if the user was the Identity or service account |
| 3 | Rebuild or move automations | Recreate under a service owner, or use the deprecated CLI knowing history and connections are lost | n/a |
| 4 | Promote community sheets people rely on | App owner approves them to base sheets (avoids unpublishing, which breaks reports) | App owner approves in the hub, or `Update-QlikObject -approved` |
| 5 | Rescue private sheets worth keeping | OAuth M2M bot with `admin.apps`: publish, or change owner | QMC App objects (Published = No) > Edit > Owner |
| 6 | Reassign apps, spaces, connections, reload schedules | Administration > Change owner; for published apps change the source app owner and republish | Change app owner, noting it can carry private sheets with it |
| 7 | Remove the user from recipient lists and filters | Subscriptions, report tasks, alerts | NPrinting recipients and filters |
| 8 | Hold, then release the license | Disable (stops dynamic re-assignment) | Deallocate Professional/Analyzer; expect the 7-day quarantine |
| 9 | Delete the user and remove from the IdP | Remove from the IdP too, or a re-login creates a new user | Delete in the QMC; first audit custom rules that reference `resource.owner` |
| 10 | Watch for failures for two weeks | "Failed to send" status; subscriptions turn off after 3 failures | NPrinting task logs; reload NPrinting metadata after object changes |

## Conclusion

The question "are orphaned community sheets still accessible?" points at the wrong layer. The sheets themselves are durable: they live inside the app, access follows the space or stream, and both platforms keep the objects after the author leaves. What actually breaks is everything tied to a *person's* ownership: reports, alerts and automations, the only identity allowed to edit or unpublish a sheet, and on Cloud the ability to find the content at all without scripting. Because a disabled owner already stops Cloud report tasks while leaving sheets intact, "disable first and decide later" is safe for sheets and unsafe for reporting. Offboarding has to treat these as separate workstreams.

The durable fix is structural rather than procedural. Sheets and objects that feed reports should be base content or master items owned by the app, and reports, NPrinting connections and automations should be owned by a service identity rather than by individuals. Customers who do this turn user departures into a licensing event. Customers who don't will run into this again with every resignation, and on Qlik Cloud the recovery tool is still an API bot rather than a button. Idea #442217, which asks for a UI to let admins change the owner of private sheets, has no confirmed delivery ([Qlik Community](https://community.qlik.com/t5/Move-to-Qlik-Cloud-Analytics/Anyone-ever-found-a-way-to-recover-private-sheets-that-belong-to/m-p/2496045)).
